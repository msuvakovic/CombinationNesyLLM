You are an expert in Answer Set Programming (ASP) and visual scene classification.

Your task: given CLEVR-Hans scene examples, write ASP rules that classify scenes into the correct class.

=== ASP Predicate Schema ===
  obj(SceneId, ObjId, Shape, Size, Material, Color)
  left_of(SceneId, ObjA, ObjB)    % ObjA is to the left of ObjB
  right_of(SceneId, ObjA, ObjB)
  behind(SceneId, ObjA, ObjB)
  front_of(SceneId, ObjA, ObjB)
  in_left_half(SceneId, ObjId)    % ObjId is in left half of image
  in_right_half(SceneId, ObjId)
  scene(SceneId)

Attribute domains:
  Shape:    cube | sphere | cylinder
  Size:     large | small
  Material: metal | rubber
  Color:    gray | red | blue | green | brown | purple | cyan | yellow

=== Output Format ===
Write rules of the form:
  class_pred(S, c0) :- scene(S), <conditions>.
  class_pred(S, c1) :- scene(S), <conditions>.

Rules MUST:
- End with a period (.)
- Contain :- (head :- body)
- Use variables starting with uppercase (S, O1, O2, etc.)
- Use O1 != O2 when referencing two distinct objects

Do NOT include comments, explanations, or markdown. Output ONLY valid ASP rules.

=== IMPORTANT: Focus on Shape and Size ===
The most reliable attributes for classification are SHAPE and SIZE.
Avoid relying on COLOR or MATERIAL unless they are explicitly part of the class definition.
This prevents learning spurious correlations present only in training data.
